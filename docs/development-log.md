### 2026-11-18 - Build split files with plate grouping

- Contributor: xychen5165
- Attempt: Built training/development/test files from the metadata. Removed 3 noisy dishes (zero energy, or 3,051 g / 3,943 kcal). Grouped repeat photos of the same plate so no plate crosses files. Tried three grouping rules:
  1. Ingredient names only (earlier ingredients a subset of later), 600 seconds or less.
  2. Ingredient weights: every earlier ingredient present in the next dish with at least the same weight (5% or 1 g), total mass not dropping (5% or 1 g).
  3. Rule 2, but also comparing with the 3 previous dishes.
- Outcome: Rule 1 missed 277 re-photographs of identical plates and linked pairs where a portion shrank; it found only 3 training dishes sharing a plate with test. Rule 3 merged groups of up to 16 photos through common single items, so it was rejected. Rule 2 was kept with a 120-second cutoff (98% of weight-matching pairs fall within it). Current counts: 2,189 training / 550 development / 506 test (839 / 219 / 208 plates); 14 training dishes removed for sharing a plate with test. No dish or plate appears in two files. Flagged but kept: 116 dishes whose energy does not match 9 × fat + 4 × carbohydrate + 4 × protein, and 31 dishes with an ingredient named "deprecated".
- Lesson and next action: Name-only matching underestimated leakage, and thresholds should be measured rather than assumed. Grouping is inferred; note this as a limitation in the report.
- Evidence: `src/build_splits.py`, `data/splits/` (README, summary.json, dropped.csv, plate_links.csv).

### 2026-11-18 - Visual review of plate links

### 2026-11-20 - Photos, median baseline, and combined plate grouping

Compared our splits with the calorie-lens dataset (a cleaned Nutrition5k on Hugging Face) and combined its plate groups with our ingredient rule: two dishes are the same plate if either method links them.

### 2026-11-21 - Regression head on CLIP features

- Contributor: xychen5165
- Attempt: Trained a linear model and the planned 512 → 128 → 5 network on the CLIP features (absolute and squared loss, seeds 0–2), then a weight decay × dropout grid.
- Outcome: The network with absolute loss scores 0.360 (energy error 74.7 kcal) against 0.737 for the median baseline; the linear model scores 0.397 and squared loss 0.372. The network overfits after about 10 epochs. Weight decay 0.001 + dropout 0.5 gives 0.354, within noise, but steadier across seeds.
- Lesson and next action: Overfitting is not the main limit; the input is. Next: food-only photos from part A, and log targets.
- Evidence: [experiments/002-clip-head-original](../experiments/002-clip-head-original/README.md), [experiments/003-regularization](../experiments/003-regularization/README.md).
