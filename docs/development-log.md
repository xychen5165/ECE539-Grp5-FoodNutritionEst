### 2026-11-18 - Build split files with plate grouping

- Contributor: xychen5165
- Attempt: Built training/development/test files from the metadata. Removed 3 noisy dishes (zero energy, or 3,051 g / 3,943 kcal). Grouped repeat photos of the same plate so no plate crosses files. Tried three grouping rules:
  1. Ingredient names only (earlier ingredients a subset of later), 600 seconds or less.
  2. Ingredient weights: every earlier ingredient present in the next dish with at least the same weight (5% or 1 g), total mass not dropping (5% or 1 g).
  3. Rule 2, but also comparing with the 3 previous dishes.
- Outcome: Rule 1 missed 277 re-photographs of identical plates and linked pairs where a portion shrank; it found only 3 training dishes sharing a plate with test. Rule 3 merged groups of up to 16 photos through common single items, so it was rejected. Rule 2 was kept with a 120-second cutoff (98% of weight-matching pairs fall within it). Current counts: 2,189 training / 550 development / 506 test (839 / 219 / 208 plates); 14 training dishes removed for sharing a plate with test. No dish or plate appears in two files. Flagged but kept: 116 dishes whose energy does not match 9 × fat + 4 × carbohydrate + 4 × protein, and 31 dishes with an ingredient named "deprecated".
- Lesson and next action: Name-only matching underestimated leakage, and thresholds should be measured rather than assumed. Grouping is inferred; note this as a limitation in the report.
- Evidence: `src/build_splits.py`, `data/splits/` (README, summary.json, dropped.csv, plate_links.csv).

### 2026-11-18 - Visual review of plate links

- Contributor: xychen5165
- Attempt: Downloaded 42 overhead photos and built a side-by-side review page of 22 dish pairs: linked pairs 61–120 seconds apart, unlinked pairs 121–600 seconds apart that pass the weight and mass checks, and two identical-label pairs about 20 minutes apart. Inspected 6 pairs.
- Outcome: Linked pair at 111 s is the same plate (correct). Unlinked pairs at 282 s (an apple; one photo in test, one in training) and 331 s (carrots) are identical photos of the same plate: missed links, one causing train/test leakage. An identical-label pair at 18 minutes appears to be the same plate; one at 21 minutes is clearly different.
- Lesson and next action: The 120-second cutoff misses re-photographs taken after a pause, and identical labels alone do not identify the same plate. Finish reviewing pairs 11–20, choose the cutoff (likely 600 seconds), and rebuild the splits.
- Evidence: [data/splits/plate_pair_review.html](../data/splits/plate_pair_review.html), generated by `src/html_plate_review.py` (photos load from Nutrition5k's public storage).

### 2026-11-20 - Photos, median baseline, and combined plate grouping

- Contributor: xychen5165
- Attempt: Downloaded all 3,262 Cafe 1 overhead photos (`src/download_photos.py`). Ran the median baseline on the development split. Compared our splits with the calorie-lens dataset (a cleaned Nutrition5k on Hugging Face) and combined its plate groups with our ingredient rule: two dishes are the same plate if either method links them.
- Outcome: All 3,245 split dishes have valid 640 × 480 photos. The two groupings miss opposite links: ours misses 153 links over 2 minutes apart, theirs misses 261 under 2 minutes. Combining them removes 79 training dishes that share a plate with test (was 14), including the apple leak. Also removed a training photo that shows a phone instead of food. New splits: 2,135 training / 538 development / 506 test. Median baseline: normalized nutrient error 0.737 (95% interval 0.672–0.812); energy error 172.1 kcal.
- Lesson and next action: An independent grouping found leaks our rule could not, and a photo-level error that label checks could not. Next: CLIP features and the regression head.
- Evidence: `src/build_splits.py`, [data/splits/README.md](../data/splits/README.md), [experiments/001-median-baseline](../experiments/001-median-baseline/README.md).

### 2026-11-21 - CLIP features for the original photos

- Contributor: xychen5165
- Attempt: Set up a Python environment (`requirements.txt`) and extracted frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 features (OpenAI weights) for all 3,262 overhead photos. Photos are padded to a square instead of center-cropped so food at the edges and the sense of scale are kept.
- Outcome: 3,262 × 512 features, no missing values. A 64-photo test ran in 5 seconds, but the full run took about 16 hours, most likely because the laptop slept or throttled.
- Lesson and next action: Keep the computer awake for long runs (or use CHTC). Next: train the linear model and the regression head on these features.
- Evidence: `src/extract_clip_features.py`, [B_nutrition_estimation/README.md](../B_nutrition_estimation/README.md).

### 2026-11-21 - Regression head on CLIP features

- Contributor: xychen5165
- Attempt: Trained a linear model and the planned 512 → 128 → 5 network on the CLIP features (absolute and squared loss, seeds 0–2), then a weight decay × dropout grid.
- Outcome: The network with absolute loss scores 0.360 (energy error 74.7 kcal) against 0.737 for the median baseline; the linear model scores 0.397 and squared loss 0.372. The network overfits after about 10 epochs. Weight decay 0.001 + dropout 0.5 gives 0.354, within noise, but steadier across seeds.
- Lesson and next action: Overfitting is not the main limit; the input is. Next: food-only photos from part A, and log targets.
- Evidence: [experiments/002-clip-head-original](../experiments/002-clip-head-original/README.md), [experiments/003-regularization](../experiments/003-regularization/README.md).
